How Hall OS works

Four operating modules. One coordinated system.

Every vendor operates on shared infrastructure, clear standards, and a connected commerce stack. Each module addresses a critical area of food hall operations: physical space, brand readiness, revenue management, and long-term growth.

01 Space OS Shared commercial infrastructure. Read more ↓ 02 Brand OS A launch-ready brand system. Read more ↓ 03 Revenue OS Unified commerce across every stall. Read more ↓ 04 Growth OS A measured path to expansion. Read more ↓
01
Space OS

Shared commercial infrastructure.

Production kitchen, cold storage, dish operations, guest seating, and the operating flow that connects them.

  • 01.A

    Capital-intensive infrastructure, already in place.

    A shared commercial kitchen, walk-in cooler, and dish operation reduce the upfront build-out burden that prevents many operators from opening independently.

  • 01.B

    A floor plan designed for peak service.

    Stall fronts, runner paths, and seat-to-stall ratios are planned around lunch and dinner rush periods, not improvised after opening.

  • 01.C

    Shared services managed centrally.

    Seating, restrooms, cleaning, and the bar program are operated by the Hub, allowing vendors to stay focused on food quality and service execution.

02
Brand OS

A launch-ready brand system.

Naming, visual identity, menus, photography, and launch materials developed in-house and owned by the vendor.

  • 02.A

    Naming, identity, and menu presentation.

    Each operator launches with a clear name, visual mark, menu board, and signage package before the first day of service.

  • 02.B

    Scheduled photography and menu content.

    Quarterly photography and dish styling keep menus, promotions, and social channels current without requiring each vendor to hire a separate marketing team.

  • 02.C

    A consistent hall-wide signage system.

    Wayfinding, stall fronts, gift-card art, and promotional templates create a cohesive destination while preserving each vendor's individual identity.

03
Revenue OS

Unified commerce across every stall.

POS, inventory, loyalty, reporting, and vendor payouts operate from a shared system, giving vendors their own view while giving management full operational visibility.

Online orders, counter tickets, and stall kitchens update the same inventory source, so item availability remains accurate across every channel.

  • One POS terminal across every stall and the bar
  • Unified inventory across online & in-person channels
  • Real-time stock synchronization to prevent overselling
  • Automatic stock adjustment on every sale
  • Live API updates push changes instantly
  • Restocking validates & updates in real time
  • Discrepancy flagging and correction workflows
  • Bulk inventory import via Excel for setup

Customer rewards, gift cards, cashback, and configurable promotions are managed across the hall without fragmented third-party tools.

  • Customer club for repeat-visit rewards
  • Engagement tools that encourage return visits
  • Native hall-wide cashback program
  • Discount engine for configurable promotions
  • Gift cards without a separate third-party plugin
  • Incentive programs for retention
  • Promotion functionality for offers & flash specials

Each stall has its own dashboard while the hall operator manages performance, fees, and payouts from a master panel.

  • Per-vendor panels based on marketplace operating models
  • Individual stall dashboards for products & sales
  • Full order timeline: payment → fulfillment
  • Real-time sales feed for automatic rent and fee deductions
  • Master operator panel across every stall
  • Financial reporting & vendor payout management
  • Multi-vendor marketplace functionality (Tier 3)
  • Vendor-separated reporting at every level
  • Multiple-domain support, one operating system
  • Automated tenant remittance without manual reconciliation

04
Growth OS

A measured path to expansion.

Operating playbooks, performance benchmarks, and expansion support for vendors ready to grow beyond the stall.

  • 04.A

    Hiring and training playbooks.

    Job descriptions, training SOPs, and food-safety templates help a small stall team grow into a more structured operation.

  • 04.B

    A graduation track with clear performance metrics.

    Vendor-level reporting on sales, food cost, ratings, and compliance creates a practical evidence trail when a stall is ready for a standalone location.

  • 04.C

    Expansion support beyond the hall.

    Co-packing guidance, label compliance, and wholesale preparation help strong products move from stall specials into retail-ready SKUs.
